1. Executive Summary

  • Therapeutic Direction & Mechanism: An agonist strategy for FGF21 is strongly supported by biological rationale and clinical proof-of-concept; FGF21 is an endocrine hepatokine that coordinates hepatic lipid catabolism, improves insulin sensitivity, and reduces liver lipotoxicity, thereby addressing the core pathophysiological drivers of metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis (MASH) (PMID 36618930, PMID 38782182).
  • Genetic Validation: While standard GWAS catalog queries lack direct case-control associations for binary MASH diagnosis, robust Mendelian randomization and pQTL studies confirm that genetically instrumented higher circulating FGF21 levels causally improve lipid profiles, lower C-reactive protein, and reduce hepatic steatosis and fibrosis markers (PMID 36208799, PMID 37302695).
  • Preclinical Proof-of-Concept: Pharmacological target activation using recombinant FGF21, long-acting Fc-fusions, PEGylated analogs, and bispecific FGFR1c/$\beta$-Klotho agonist antibodies consistently resolves steatohepatitis, normalizes transaminases, induces adipose browning, and represses profibrotic extracellular matrix genes across rodent and non-human primate models (PMID 41150667, PMID 39463193, PMID 33421947).
  • Safety & Target Liability: gnomAD constraint demonstrates evolutionary tolerance to heterozygous loss of function ($\text{LOEUF} = 1.54$, $\text{pLI} = 3.34 \times 10^{-5}$), confirming non-essentiality; primary on-target liabilities identified in gain-of-function models are exposure-dependent bone mineral density reduction via PPAR-$\gamma$ potentiation and endocrine/somatotropic axis modulation at supratherapeutic levels (PMID 22315431, PMID 30389664), alongside a $\sim$1.3-fold safety attrition risk based on 11 IntAct physical interaction partners.
  • Competitive Landscape: The investigational pipeline is exceptionally advanced with no currently approved agents but three distinct assets in Phase 3 development (pegozafermin, efruxifermin, efimosfermin alfa) and multiple Phase 2 programs across 16 registered clinical trials on ClinicalTrials.gov.
  • Tractability Verdict: FGF21 exhibits high overall tractability as a therapeutic target for MASH, supported by definitive clinical-stage antifibrotic and steatosis-reversing efficacy; successful development requires optimizing pharmacokinetic half-life while monitoring bone turnover and reproductive axis biomarkers.

Extensive preclinical interventional and genetic studies provide robust proof-of-concept for an agonist strategy targeting FGF21 in metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis (MASH) and steatotic liver disease (MASLD). In translational dietary models of MASH, administration of recombinant FGF21, long-acting analogs, or receptor mimetics consistently resolves steatosis, attenuates lobular inflammation, and arrests fibrogenesis. In AMLN diet-induced obese MASH mice with biopsy-confirmed disease, monotherapy with the long-acting fatty acid-modified FGF21 analog zalfermin (0.2 mg/kg QD) or low-dose zalfermin (0.05 mg/kg) combined with semaglutide achieved substantial body weight loss (-16% to -18%), normalized plasma ALT and AST, depleted liver triglycerides, reduced NAFLD Activity Scores (NAS), and suppressed extracellular matrix (ECM) remodeling genes (such as Col1a1, Col3a1, Col4a1, and Loxl2) independent of body weight loss (PMID 41150667). Similarly, in high-fat diet-fed $Ldlr^{-/-}\text{.Leiden}$ mice, the bispecific anti-FGFR1c/$\beta$-Klotho agonist antibody bFKB1 (5 mg/kg weekly) reduced body weight (-21%), plasma insulin (-80%), plasma ALT (-79%), liver weight (-43%), macrovesicular steatosis (-34%), and hepatic microvesicular steatosis (-100%), alongside a 49% reduction in de novo collagen synthesis, broad inactivation of hepatic TGF-$\beta$/SMAD signaling, adipose browning (8.5-fold UCP1 induction), and a 38% reduction in aortic atherosclerotic lesion area (PMID 39463193). Engineered modalities—including a GLP-1/Fc-FGF21 dual agonist (drug pipeline) in $ob/ob$ DIO-MASH mice (PMID 33421947), tetra-specific C-192 targeting GLP-1/GCG/FGF21/IL-1RA in MCD-fed mice (PMID 38004466), and surface FGF21-displaying extracellular vesicles (223/F-EVs) in CDAHFD mice (PMID 40209593)—replicated pronounced steatosis and inflammation improvements. In a multigenerational maternal Western diet model, wild-type FGF21 (0.3 mg/kg BID) reversed steatohepatitis and restored hepatic mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation (OXPHOS) and biogenesis (Pgc1α, Nrf1) (PMID 40496439). Mechanistically, FGF21 signaling through FGFRs/$\beta$-Klotho recruits serine/threonine phosphatase PPP6C to dephosphorylate TSC2, thereby repressing hepatic mTORC1 and driving TFE3/Lipin1 nuclear translocation (PMID 39947331). In toxicant-associated steatohepatitis, recombinant FGF21 suppressed hepatic lipocalin-2 (LCN2) overexpression, reducing steatosis and iron overload (PMID 36012166). Engineered long-acting fusion variants such as PsTag-FGF21(V169L) (PMID 38740172) and PsTag600-FGF21 (PMID 29859019; full-text retrieval failed; findings based on abstract only; additional body-text content may have been missed) also demonstrated enhanced pharmacokinetic stability, reduced hepatocyte apoptosis, and decreased IL17A-driven inflammation.

Loss-of-function and gain-of-function models reinforce this mechanism: Fgf21 knockout mice fed high-fat, high-carbohydrate, or methionine-choline-deficient diets exhibit accelerated, severe steatohepatitis, elevated free fatty acids, exacerbated macrophage activation, and heightened oncogenic Wnt/$\beta$-catenin activation (PMID 27293995; full-text retrieval failed; findings based on abstract only; additional body-text content may have been missed; PMID 41299066; PMID 38599281). Conversely, adult-onset adipocyte-specific FGF21 overexpression in HFD-fed mice extended median survival to 2.225 years (with individuals reaching 3.3 years), sustained whole-body insulin sensitivity, eliminated microvesicular steatosis, and remodeled visceral adipose tissue by reducing lipotoxic ceramides and promoting anti-inflammatory M2 macrophage polarization (PMID 40527315). While preclinical models highlight occasional on-target safety considerations—such as potential bone resorption under non-targeted high-dose overexpression (PMID 40594911, PMID 39651131, PMID 36594101) or elevated circulating FGF21 marking an endogenous "FGF21-resistant" state in severe obesity (PMID 38894596, PMID 38710230, PMID 33758421)—pharmacological FGF21 agonism successfully overrides endogenous resistance and delivers comprehensive hepatoprotective, anti-inflammatory, and metabolic efficacy across diverse preclinical species.

Section Summary

Preclinical evidence provides strong validation for an agonist therapeutic strategy targeting FGF21 in MASH. Although standardized high-throughput International Mouse Phenotyping Consortium (IMPC) knockout data were unavailable for Fgf21 (Section 2.4), extensive literature-derived genetic and interventional models (Section 2.5) demonstrate that genetic ablation (Fgf21 KO) accelerates hepatic steatosis, inflammatory infiltration, and cellular injury, directly supporting the rationale for target activation. Recombinant FGF21, long-acting analogs (e.g., zalfermin, efruxifermin mimetics), bispecific receptor agonists (bFKB1), and multi-agonist fusion proteins consistently resolve steatohepatitis, normalize transaminases, enhance mitochondrial biogenesis, and downregulate profibrotic extracellular matrix gene expression programs across dietary and toxicant-induced rodent models. Together, these robust pharmacological and genetic data confirm high preclinical tractability for FGF21 pathway activation in MASH.

3.2.4 Isoform Biology & Therapeutic Selectivity

  • Isoform Landscape: Canonical FGF21 encodes a single, secreted 209-amino-acid polypeptide containing an N-terminal signaling domain that interacts with FGF receptors (FGFR1c, FGFR2c, FGFR3c) and a C-terminal tail that binds the obligate coreceptor $\beta$-Klotho (KLB) .
  • Alternative Isoforms in Literature: The provided genetic and clinical literature does not document functional alternative splicing isoforms with divergent pathological or protective roles in MASH.
  • Selectivity Implications: Because MASH protection is driven by canonical endocrine FGF21 signaling through the hepatic and adipose FGFR1c/KLB receptor complex , an isoform-selective targeting strategy within the ligand itself is neither applicable nor necessary. Instead, therapeutic selectivity focuses on engineering ligand analogues (or bispecific receptor agonists) optimized for specific FGFR/KLB co-receptor geometries to maximize hepatic/metabolic efficacy while minimizing off-target FGFR activation .

3.3 Published Genetic Association Evidence (PubMed)

Genetic and Mendelian randomization studies provide key human evidence supporting the causal involvement of FGF21 signaling in hepatic steatosis, metabolic dysfunction, and liver injury:

  1. Mendelian Randomization in Steatohepatitis and Liver Biomarkers: Larsson et al. (PMID 36208799, PMID 37023631) performed GWAS and two-sample MR of circulating FGF21, demonstrating that genetically predicted circulating FGF21 concentrations exert strong causal effects on improving circulating lipid profiles, reducing C-reactive protein, and improving biomarkers of liver function, including markers of liver fibrosis and steatohepatitis (NASH/MASH). Giontella et al. (PMID 37302695) leveraged the FGF21 missense variant rs739320 (associated with MRI-derived liver fat) as an instrument, confirming broad metabolic and renoprotective benefits.
  2. Reverse Causality and Steatosis Resistance: He et al. (PMID 40245929) conducted bidirectional MR across metabolic traits, finding that non-alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD/MASLD: $\beta = -0.047, p = 0.006$), obesity ($\beta = 0.087, p = 0.009$), and type 2 diabetes ($\beta = 0.071, p < 0.001$) causally regulate circulating FGF21 levels, consistent with an endogenous compensatory upregulation in insulin- and FGF21-resistant metabolic states.
  3. Alcohol-Related Liver Injury and Ingestion Behavior: Rosoff et al. (PMID 41747774) demonstrated via multivariable and receptor-focused MR (evaluating KLB and FGFR1/2/3) that genetically higher FGF21 levels reduce problematic alcohol use ($\beta = -0.097, p = 6.13 \times 10^{-6}$) and protect against alcohol-related liver disease (ALD), paralleling non-human primate work by Flippo et al. (PMID 35108517) demonstrating KLB-dependent central regulation of ingestive behavior.
  4. Pleiotropic and Null Associations: Multiple studies identified FGF21 locus variants influencing dietary carbohydrate and sugar preferences (PMID 41056811, PMID 31190057), while showing null or weak causal associations with primary hypertension, cardiomyopathy, or peripheral vascular diseases in MR screens (PMID 40245929).

3.4 Integrated Human Genetics Verdict

Human genetic support for FGF21 as a causal therapeutic target in metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis (MASH) is MODERATE; while direct, clean case-control GWAS signals for binary MASH diagnosis are absent in standard catalog queries (which are dominated by off-topic pQTLs, lipid fractions, and dietary preference signals), robust Mendelian randomization studies instrumenting FGF21 variants consistently demonstrate causal protection against hepatic steatosis, elevated liver enzymes, and fibrosis.

Gene constraint analysis in gnomAD indicates complete evolutionary tolerance to heterozygous loss of function ($\text{LOEUF} = 1.54, \text{pLI} \approx 0$), confirming that FGF21 is not an essential housekeeping gene, though its 11 IntAct physical interaction partners place it in a moderate clinical safety attrition risk band ($\sim$1.3-fold risk) requiring careful monitoring of systemic receptor engagement.

Because FGF21 functions as a single canonical full-length hormone without disease-specific pathological splice isoforms, therapeutic development does not require isoform-selective inhibition; instead, human genetics fully supports the ongoing agonist strategy to enhance metabolic flux, reduce hepatic lipotoxicity, and reverse steatohepatitis.

Section Summary

Human genetic evidence and tissue expression profiling provide coherent support for the therapeutic activation of FGF21 in MASH. Although binary disease GWAS signals are obscured by the polygenic background of steatohepatitis, Mendelian randomization studies consistently validate that genetically higher circulating FGF21 confers causal hepatoprotection, reduced hepatic steatosis, and lower fibrosis scores. Complete loss-of-function tolerance in gnomAD ($\text{LOEUF} = 1.54$) combined with liver-restricted baseline synthesis confirms that FGF21 is a non-housekeeping endocrine metabolic regulator, supporting the safety and biological rationale of an agonist approach.

5. Target Safety Evaluation

5.1 Loss-of-Function Safety Profile

Overall verdict

STRONG support for the activation hypothesis, as human genetic variation and preclinical loss-of-function (LoF) models demonstrate that endogenous FGF21 deficiency promotes hepatic steatosis, dyslipidemia, uncontrolled preference for simple sugars and alcohol, and increased vulnerability to inflammatory and vascular tissue injury, validating that therapeutic FGF21 agonism directly addresses the root metabolic drivers of MASLD and cardiometabolic disease (PMID 39450972, PMID 41238906, PMID 26654352).

Adverse effects by system

Hepatic and Metabolic System Loss of FGF21 function severely disrupts hepatic fatty acid oxidation, lipid partitioning, and whole-body glucose homeostasis (PMID 26654352). Human carriers of alleles that decrease FGF21 expression or function exhibit higher baseline triglyceride levels, increased hepatic fat accumulation, and impaired insulin sensitivity. Mechanistically, FGF21 acts as a master endocrine and autocrine regulator of hepatic lipid catabolism and white adipose tissue glucose uptake; its absence diminishes mitochondrial fatty acid oxidation and blunts diet-induced thermogenic responses (PMID 26654352, PMID 31012296). This metabolic impairment presents with moderate severity and is fully reversible through dietary modification or pharmacological FGF21 replacement. This pathway represents the primary therapeutic target of FGF21 agonism, wherein pharmacological activation reverses the steatotic phenotype seen in LoF states.

Central Nervous System and Ingestive Behavior FGF21 LoF leads to altered neurobehavioral regulation of macronutrient preference, specifically manifesting as an increased craving and consumption of simple sugars and alcohol (PMID 41238906, PMID 39450972). Mechanistically, liver-derived FGF21 crosses the blood-brain barrier to act on FGF21 receptor complexes in the hypothalamus and reward centers to suppress sweet and alcohol preference; failure of this endocrine liver-to-brain signaling pathway leaves carbohydrate appetitive signaling unrestrained (PMID 41238906). This behavioral phenotype is mild-to-moderate in severity, contributing chronically to metabolic syndrome and obesity, and is functionally reversible upon restoring central FGF21 signaling. This behavioral regulation pathway shares direct mechanistic overlap with the metabolic-protective actions of FGF21, operating even in common heterozygous hypomorphic variants (PMID 39450972).

Pancreatic and Gastrointestinal System Deficiency in FGF21 signaling increases susceptibility to pancreatic acinar cell injury and exacerbates pancreatitis (PMID 26654352, PMID 32029920). Endogenous FGF21 is highly expressed in the exocrine pancreas, where it functions as an essential autocrine/paracrine proteostatic and anti-inflammatory factor that shields acinar cells from secretagogue stress, lipotoxicity, and chemical insults (PMID 26654352, PMID 37848164). In the absence of intact FGF21 function, loss of this protective counter-regulatory mechanism permits unrestrained acinar necrosis, local inflammation, and elevated risk of pancreatitis episodes. The severity of acute pancreatitis episodes driven by target loss can be severe and potentially life-threatening; while early inflammatory episodes are reversible, recurrent or chronic severe pancreatitis results in irreversible parenchymal fibrosis and exocrine insufficiency.

Cardiovascular and Vascular System FGF21 LoF promotes accelerated vascular inflammation, endothelial dysfunction, and exaggerated hypertensive responses under pressor stress (PMID 29706566, PMID 41198096). Mechanistically, FGF21 deficiency prevents the compensatory induction of the angiotensin-converting enzyme 2 (ACE2)/angiotensin-(1-7) axis in adipose and renal tissues, leaving angiotensin II-mediated vasoconstriction and oxidative vascular damage unchecked (PMID 29706566). Furthermore, loss of FGF21 blunts cardiac-protective stress signaling during metabolic overload, predisposing the myocardium to adverse remodeling (PMID 26654352, PMID 41198096). The severity of cardiovascular impairment is moderate, presenting as accelerated hypertension and microvascular damage, and is reversible through restoration of FGF21-dependent ACE2 signaling or downstream renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system antagonism.

Musculoskeletal System Under basal physiological conditions, human FGF21 LoF has minimal overt impact on skeletal muscle architecture but impairs systemic stress-induced energy repartitioning. While endogenous FGF21 is normally expressed at very low levels in healthy muscle, it is markedly induced as a stress myokine during mitochondrial and metabolic stress (PMID 26654352). In contrasting pathological states such as severe mitochondrial myopathy, complete loss of FGF21 signaling prevents muscle catabolism and systemic wasting, indicating that endogenous FGF21 acts as a mediator of muscle atrophy downstream of unresolved endoplasmic reticulum and mitochondrial unfolded protein responses (PMID 28552492, PMID 31208084). The phenotypic severity in muscle is mild under baseline states but can be severe in hypercatabolic stress states; the associated metabolic shifts are generally reversible.

Protective effects of LoF

FGF21 LoF demonstrates protective phenotypes specifically in the context of hypercatabolic states, cachexia, and musculoskeletal aging:

  1. Protection against Mitochondrial Stress-Induced Muscle Atrophy and Cachexia: Ablation or loss of FGF21 prevents the systemic muscle wasting, precocious epithelial senescence, and premature lethality triggered by severe skeletal muscle mitochondrial dysfunction, such as that caused by OPA1 ablation (PMID 28552492, PMID 31208084). Mechanistically, this directly overlaps with the catabolic, energy-expending pathway mediated by endocrine FGF21; while activation of this pathway is therapeutically beneficial for clearing excess lipids in MASLD, unconstrained high-level activation drives systemic muscle and adipose catabolism.
  2. Preservation of Bone Mass under Hepatic Stress: Elevated FGF21 signaling acts as a hepatic endocrine driver of bone resorption and osteoporosis in chronic metabolic disease models (PMID 40594911, PMID 38902808). Consequently, lower baseline FGF21 activity or loss of function protects against excessive osteoclastic bone resorption, an adverse liability that requires careful monitoring when administering high-dose pharmacological FGF21 agonists (PMID 41198096).

Therapeutic window: heterozygous vs. homozygous LoF

Heterozygous common hypomorphic variants (such as rs838133 and rs2548957) cause subtle, dose-dependent reductions in FGF21 activity, which manifest clinically as mild increases in sweet and alcohol intake, modest elevations in circulating triglycerides, and subtle metabolic thriftiness without precipitating severe organ failure (PMID 39450972, PMID 31012296). In contrast, complete homozygous loss of FGF21 signaling or its obligate co-receptor β-Klotho results in severe metabolic and exocrine vulnerabilities, including heightened susceptibility to severe pancreatitis and marked exacerbation of steatohepatitis and hypertensive vascular injury under metabolic stress (PMID 39450972, PMID 32029920, PMID 29706566).

For an AGONIST therapeutic strategy, this genetic spectrum demonstrates that full baseline restoration or moderate pharmacological activation readily rescues the hepatic, metabolic, and behavioral deficits without engaging the excessive catabolic mechanisms seen in severe GoF or hyper-elevated states. Because the protective benefits of LoF (avoidance of bone resorption and muscle catabolism) only become relevant at super-physiological GoF concentrations, a well-defined therapeutic window exists: moderate-dose FGF21 agonism successfully reverses MASLD, hypertriglyceridemia, and insulin resistance while remaining well below the threshold that induces bone loss or cachexia (PMID 38782182, PMID 41198096).

Diseases caused or worsened by FGF21 overactivity

A clinician examining an individual with chronic FGF21 hyperactivation would observe progressive reduction in bone mineral density, cortical thinning, elevated trabecular bone loss, and increased susceptibility to fragility fractures. At the molecular level, circulating FGF21 engages the FGFR1–β-Klotho receptor complex on bone marrow mesenchymal stem cells, potentiating the transcriptional activity of peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor gamma (PPAR-γ); this biases mesenchymal differentiation toward the adipogenic lineage while simultaneously repressing Runt-related transcription factor 2 (Runx2)-driven osteoblastogenesis. Knock-in or transgenic animal model (transgenic mice overexpressing FGF21, Wei Wei et al., PMID 22315431; Guillaume J Trusz, PMID 38991938). The therapeutic challenge is that the PPAR-γ modulation and metabolic reprogramming that clear hepatic triglycerides directly drive marrow adiposity and osteoblast suppression.

A clinician evaluating pediatric or juvenile subjects under persistent FGF21 overactivity would observe growth deceleration, reduced crown-rump length, delayed skeletal maturation, and marked reduction in circulating insulin-like growth factor 1 (IGF-1) concentrations. Mechanistically, sustained FGF21 elevation inhibits growth hormone receptor (GHR) downstream signaling within hepatocytes by blunting Janus kinase 2 (JAK2)-dependent phosphorylation of signal transducer and activator of transcription 5 (STAT5), which represses hepatic IGF1 gene transcription and provokes systemic growth hormone resistance at the epiphyseal growth plate. Knock-in or transgenic animal model (transgenic mice overexpressing FGF21 and hepatic HRD1-deficient mice exhibiting unconstrained CREBH-mediated FGF21 production, Juncheng Wei et al., PMID 30389664). This growth suppression represents an evolutionary survival adaptation that reallocates energy away from somatic growth during periods of severe nutrient deprivation.

A clinician would detect secondary amenorrhea or oligomenorrhea, anovulatory infertility, attenuated luteinizing hormone (LH) pulsatility, and blunted preovulatory LH surges. Systemic FGF21 overactivation acts centrally within the hypothalamic-pituitary-gonadal axis to suppress kisspeptin-secreting neurons and gonadotropin-releasing hormone (GnRH) release, preventing the pituitary gonadotrope activation necessary for ovulation. Knock-in or transgenic animal model (hepatic HRD1-knockout mice with constitutive FGF21 secretion, Juncheng Wei et al., PMID 30389664). This anovulatory phenotype represents an evolutionary mechanism that limits energy expenditure on reproduction during prolonged starvation or extreme metabolic stress.

A clinician monitoring patients or animal models with elevated FGF21 would record diurnal rest-activity fragmentation, phase shifts in nocturnal locomotor behavior, and disrupted circadian metabolic rhythms. FGF21 crosses the blood-brain barrier and binds FGFR1–β-Klotho coreceptor complexes localized within the suprachiasmatic nucleus (SCN) and basomedial hypothalamus, altering the rhythmic transcription of canonical clock genes including BMAL1 (ARNTL) and PER2. Knock-in or transgenic animal model (transgenic and HRD1-deleted mice exhibiting sustained FGF21 elevation, Juncheng Wei et al., PMID 30389664).

Selectivity and isoform considerations

The physiological and pathological actions of FGF21 are mediated by the canonical, full-length 181-amino-acid circulating polypeptide, which requires the single-pass transmembrane coreceptor β-Klotho (KLB) in complex with FGF receptor 1c (FGFR1c), FGFR2c, or FGFR3c to initiate intracellular signaling (Guillaume J Trusz, PMID 38991938; Masaru Katoh, PMID 27245147). Pathological GoF phenotypes stem from excessive signaling of the intact mature hormone rather than alternative splice variants. For agonist drug design, therapeutic entities (such as long-acting Fc-fusions, PEGylated analogs, or bispecific FGFR1c/KLB antibodies) must maintain strict integrity of the β-Klotho-interaction domain to avoid off-target, Klotho-independent pan-FGFR activation.

Dose–response and target engagement implications

Phenotypic outcomes from genetic models demonstrate that FGF21 hyperactivation produces a graded, exposure-dependent response rather than an all-or-none switch (Wei Wei et al., PMID 22315431; Juncheng Wei et al., PMID 30389664). Heterozygous GoF states or moderate pharmacological elevations demonstrate substantial reductions in hepatic steatosis, serum triglycerides, and body weight while largely sparing skeletal integrity and reproductive competence. In contrast, massive transgene-driven overexpression or high-dose continuous agonist exposures surpass the homeostatic safety threshold, precipitating progressive trabecular bone loss, growth deceleration, and anovulation (Wei Wei et al., PMID 22315431; Juncheng Wei et al., PMID 30389664). Target engagement threshold: Unable to determine from available evidence. The single most important experiment to define the therapeutic window is a multi-dose escalation study of a long-acting FGF21 agonist in adult non-human primates, simultaneously evaluating non-invasive biomarkers of metabolic efficacy (hepatic proton density fat fraction by MRI-PDFF, serum triglycerides) alongside dynamic bone histomorphometry, high-resolution peripheral quantitative computed tomography (HR-pQCT), and sex hormone profiling.

Overall Safety Summary

In evaluating an agonist therapeutic strategy for FGF21, genetic loss-of-function and gain-of-function data must be interpreted in reverse of an inhibitor paradigm: LoF phenotypes (Section 5.1) validate that endogenous target deficiency precipitates hepatic steatosis, hypertriglyceridemia, and tissue vulnerability, while GoF models (Section 5.2) preview potential on-target liabilities arising from supratherapeutic receptor engagement. In gnomAD, FGF21 is classified as TOLERANT to loss of function, with a LOEUF score of 1.541 and a pLI of $3.34 \times 10^{-5}$, indicating that natural heterozygous LoF is evolutionarily neutral; among 20 high-confidence LoF variants identified in gnomAD, none occur at meaningful population frequency ($\text{AF} > 0.0001$; maximum $\text{AF} = 6.17 \times 10^{-6}$). However, network analysis reveals that FGF21 possesses 11 physical interaction partners in IntAct ($\text{MI} > 0.42$), placing it into the 11–20 partner band. Targets within this connectivity range have been reported to exhibit an estimated $\sim$1.3-fold higher odds of clinical trial termination due to safety reasons relative to minimally connected targets (Razuvayevskaya et al., Nat Genet 2024, doi:10.1038/s41588-024-01854-z). Because the LOEUF of 1.541 confirms target non-essentiality while GoF models highlight exposure-dependent bone mineral loss (via PPAR-$\gamma$ potentiation) and reproductive/growth axis modulation, the overall safety profile for an AGONIST strategy represents a low-to-moderate, exposure-manageable safety concern that requires careful dose titration and clinical monitoring of bone mineral density and endocrine biomarkers.

Cardiovascular hemodynamic elevations and reproductive axis suppression represent secondary, manageable liabilities, while gastrointestinal intolerance is high-incidence but low-impact. The single piece of evidence that would most dramatically alter this grid is long-term, multi-year DEXA bone mineral density and fracture incidence data from ongoing Phase 3 trials of pegozafermin or efruxifermin, which would establish whether preclinical bone loss translates into clinically meaningful skeletal fragility in humans.

8. Conclusions

All major biomedical evidence pillars—including structural biology, preclinical pharmacological models, human genetics/Mendelian randomization, and clinical pipeline validation—are fully populated for FGF21, while standardized IMPC knockout data are absent. A definitive GO recommendation is issued for developing FGF21 agonists for metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis (MASH). This recommendation is justified by robust clinical and Mendelian randomization proof-of-concept demonstrating profound steatosis resolution, transaminase normalization, and histological fibrosis improvement. The primary outstanding developmental risk is managing potential on-target bone mineral density reduction and endocrine/reproductive axis modulation at high sustained exposures, which can be mitigated through optimized dosing schedules and dynamic clinical bone biomarker surveillance.
