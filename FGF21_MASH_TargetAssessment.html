1. Executive Summary

  • Therapeutic Direction & Rationale: An agonist therapeutic strategy is strongly supported for FGF21 in metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis (MASH), as endogenous FGF21 functions as a hepatoprotective endocrine hormone stimulating fatty acid oxidation, resolving lipotoxicity, and reversing steatohepatitis (PMID 26654352, PMID 36102936).
  • Human Genetic Support: Mendelian randomization and instrumental variable studies confirm that genetically higher FGF21 activity causally correlates with improved lipid profiles, reduced hepatic fat accumulation, decreased systemic inflammation, and lower risk of liver fibrosis (PMID 36208799, PMID 41747774, PMID 37302695), despite primary diagnostic endpoints being unrepresented in standard GWAS Catalog clean annotations.
  • Preclinical Proof-of-Concept: Preclinical target engagement across dietary and genetic rodent models confirms therapeutic efficacy, with recombinant FGF21, bispecific antibodies (bFKB1), long-acting analogues (zalfermin), and multi-specific constructs resolving steatohepatitis, reducing transaminases by up to 79%, and suppressing collagen deposition (PMID 40496439, PMID 39463193, PMID 41150667).
  • Safety Profile & On-Target Liabilities: Gene constraint analysis demonstrates complete tolerance to loss of function in the general population (gnomAD LOEUF = 1.541, pLI = $3.34 \times 10^{-5}$), while gain-of-function models identify potential on-target liabilities of sustained supra-physiological exposure, specifically bone mineral density reduction mediated by PPAR-$\gamma$ potentiation in bone marrow and neuroendocrine axis disruption (PMID 22315431, PMID 30389664).
  • Competitive Landscape: While no FGF21 therapies are currently approved, the investigational landscape is highly active and advanced, featuring multiple Phase 3 biologics engineered for extended half-life (efruxifermin, pegozafermin, efimosfermin alfa) across 16 registered clinical trials.
  • Overall Tractability Verdict: FGF21 exhibits high biological and pharmacological tractability as an agonist target for MASH, supported by robust human causal genetics, clear preclinical validation, and high structural feasibility for engineered protein and antibody therapeutics.

2. Target Biology & Mechanism of Action

2.1 Gene & Protein Overview

FGF21 (Fibroblast growth factor 21) is a 209-amino acid secreted peptide hormone belonging to the endocrine FGF19 subfamily. It lacks canonical transmembrane helices and possesses a structurally flexible C-terminal disordered region critical for co-receptor binding.

2.2 Signalling Mechanism

Upstream cellular stress response and metabolic transcription factors directly regulate FGF21 gene expression. In SIGNOR signalling interactions, transcription factors ATF4 (PMID 22233381), ATF2 (PMID 25055037), and TFEB (PMID 32978159) up-regulate FGF21 expression in response to amino acid deprivation, energetic stress, and lysosomal/autophagic demands. Conversely, DNMT3A down-regulates FGF21 through transcriptional repression (PMID 29091029).

Downstream, FGF21 acts systemically as an endocrine factor requiring the obligate transmembrane co-receptor $\beta$-Klotho (KLB) in complex with FGF receptors (predominantly FGFR1c) to trigger downstream intracellular cascades. In canonical pathway annotations, FGF21 participates in cellular hexose transport (Reactome R-HSA-189200) and the assembly of active LPL and LIPC lipase complexes (Reactome R-HSA-8963889). Open Targets association scores strongly corroborate the involvement of FGF21 in metabolic pathophysiology, demonstrating high scores for obesity due to melanocortin 4 receptor deficiency (0.6221), type 2 diabetes mellitus (0.5953), hepatocellular carcinoma (0.5946), metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (0.5910), and metabolic syndrome (0.5815).

Preclinical in vivo and in vitro models robustly validate an agonism strategy for FGF21 in metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis (MASH). Loss-of-function (LoF) evidence confirms target hypothesis validity: genetic deletion of Fgf21 in mice exacerbates high-fat and methionine/choline-deficient diet-induced MASH, leading to elevated NAFLD Activity Scores (NAS), severe steatosis, aggravated inflammatory cell infiltration, unrestrained Wnt/$\beta$-catenin activation, and heightened susceptibility to hepatocellular carcinoma (PMID 41299066, PMID 27293995 (findings based on abstract only due to full-text retrieval failure)). Conversely, interventional gain-of-function (GoF) and pharmacological agonism models demonstrate profound therapeutic efficacy across diverse preclinical settings. Administration of wild-type recombinant FGF21 (0.3 mg/kg twice daily) in male mice challenged with multigenerational Western diet significantly resolved steatohepatitis and restored hepatic mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation (OXPHOS) subunit expression (PMID 40496439). In high-fat diet-fed $Ldlr^{-/-}\text{.Leiden}$ mice, the bispecific anti-FGFR1c/$\beta$-Klotho agonist antibody bFKB1 (5 mg/kg weekly) lowered body weight by 21%, plasma ALT by 79%, triglycerides by 76%, macrovesicular steatosis by 34%, microvesicular steatosis by 100%, and intrahepatic inflammation by 74%, while reducing de novo collagen synthesis by 49% and inactivating profibrotic TGF-$\beta$/SMAD cascades (PMID 39463193). Similarly, the long-acting fatty acid-modified FGF21 analogue zalfermin (0.2 mg/kg QD), alone or in low-dose combination with semaglutide, produced robust improvements in liver enzymes, steatosis, lobular inflammation, and body weight-independent suppression of extracellular matrix gene expression in biopsy-confirmed AMLN DIO-MASH mice (PMID 41150667). Engineered multi-specific constructs, such as the GLP-1-Fc-FGF21 dual agonist drug pipeline (PMID 33421947), the tetra-specific C-192 (PMID 38004466), and surface-engineered extracellular vesicles carrying FGF21 (PMID 40209593), consistently reversed steatohepatitis, lowered transaminases, and reduced NAS scores in ob/ob and CDAHFD mouse models. Long-term GoF evaluations, including adult-onset adipocyte-specific FGF21 overexpression, extended median survival up to 3.3 years on a high-fat diet, cleared liver steatosis, and suppressed toxic ceramide accumulation without inducing bone loss or muscle catabolism (PMID 40527315). Mechanistically, FGF21 acts directly via hepatic FGFRs/$\beta$-Klotho to engage PPP6C phosphatase, inhibiting mTORC1 and dephosphorylating TSC2 (PMID 39947331), while also reducing macrophage inflammation through autophagy-mediated HIF-1$\alpha$ degradation (PMID 38599281) and suppressing lipocalin-2 in toxicant-induced steatohepatitis (PMID 36012166). In contrast, FAP-mediated cleavage rapidly inactivates native human FGF21 at residue Pro171, justifying the development of cleavage-resistant analogues, Fc/PEG fusions, and FAP inhibitors such as BR103354 (PMID 33277568, PMID 40803572 (findings based on abstract only), PMID 29859019 (findings based on abstract only)).

Section Summary

Target tractability for an FGF21 agonism strategy in MASH is strongly supported by convergent preclinical evidence, despite the absence of standardized phenotypic calls in the IMPC repository (Unable to locate source). Loss-of-function genetic mouse models consistently demonstrate that endogenous FGF21 deficiency accelerates steatohepatitis, impairs lipid handling, and exacerbates fibrosis, confirming the protective physiological requirement of this pathway. Pharmacological target engagement across multiple therapeutic modalities—including native recombinant FGF21, receptor-selective bispecific antibodies (bFKB1), long-acting analogues (zalfermin, efruxifermin, pegozafermin), multi-targeted fusions, and FAP inhibitors—demonstrates consistent resolution of steatohepatitis, reduction in lipid accumulation, improvement in transaminases, and suppression of fibrogenic programs across rodent, non-human primate, and in vitro systems.

3.2 gnomAD Constraint Analysis

3.2.1 Gene-Level Constraint

Assessment of FGF21 (ENSG00000105550) in gnomAD v4 reveals:

  • pLI: $3.34 \times 10^{-5}$
  • LOEUF ($oe_lof_upper$): 1.541
  • o/e LoF: 0.969 ($n_{\text{obs}} = 13$, $n_{\text{exp}} = 13.41$)
  • Missence $Z$-score: -0.792; LoF $Z$-score: 0.095

An observed-to-expected LoF ratio of 0.969 and a LOEUF score of 1.541 (> 1.0) indicate that FGF21 is completely unconstrained and highly tolerant to loss of function in the general population. In the context of an agonist therapeutic strategy, tolerance to loss of function does not indicate an inherent safety hazard for pathway activation. Rather, lack of selective pressure against natural LoF suggests that physiological baseline suppression of FGF21 is viable in healthy humans, while therapeutic agonism aims to provide supra-physiological or restored metabolic signaling. Potential on-target liabilities of receptor hyperactivation (such as sympathetic activation, bone turnover alterations, or gastrointestinal disturbances) must be monitored based on receptor distribution rather than baseline LoF intolerance.

3.2.2 Natural LoF Variant Landscape

A total of 20 high-confidence loss-of-function variants (stop-gained, splice site donor/acceptor, frameshift) pass quality filters in gnomAD v4. All 20 variants are ultra-rare, with allele frequencies (AF) ranging from $6.84 \times 10^{-7}$ to $6.17 \times 10^{-6}$ (e.g., stop_gained variant 19-48758197-C-T, AC = 9, AF = $6.17 \times 10^{-6}$; 19-48756468-G-T, AC = 3, AF = $2.06 \times 10^{-6}$). No natural LoF variants have an AF > 0.0001 (0.01%).

3.2.3 IntAct Interaction Network (Safety-Attrition Context)

In the IntAct database, FGF21 possesses 11 physical interaction partners with a molecular interaction (MI) score > 0.42 (out of 39 total fetched interactions, 33 passing the MI cutoff). This network connectivity metric provides an orthogonal, trial-level safety consideration indicating pleiotropic signaling through FGFR/co-receptor complexes across multiple tissue compartments.

3.2.4 Isoform Biology & Therapeutic Selectivity

Canonical human FGF21 encodes a single, secreted endocrine polypeptide that acts as a circulating hormone requiring $\beta$-Klotho (KLB) alongside fibroblast growth factor receptors (primarily FGFR1c) to facilitate high-affinity signaling . The literature provided does not identify alternative functional splice isoforms with distinct pathogenic versus protective activities in MASH. Thus, therapeutic agonist strategies focus on full-length recombinant analogs or engineered Fc-fusion / PEGylated mimetics that retain balanced KLB/FGFR1c activation rather than isoform-selective designs.

3.3 Published Genetic Association Evidence (PubMed)

Published Mendelian randomization (MR) and genomic studies provide strong, causally directed human genetic evidence supporting the beneficial metabolic and hepatoprotective effects of elevated FGF21 activity:

  • Mendelian Randomization in Steatohepatitis and Liver Injury: Larsson et al. (PMID 36208799, Corrigendum PMID 37023631) conducted discovery GWAS and two-sample MR, demonstrating that genetically predicted circulating FGF21 levels causally correlate with improved circulating lipid profiles, reduced alcohol intake, reduced systemic inflammation (C-reactive protein), and improved liver function biomarkers, including protection against steatohepatitis/fibrosis. Rosoff et al. (PMID 41747774) integrated MR across UK Biobank, FinnGen, Million Veteran Program, and GenomALC, demonstrating that genetically elevated FGF21 signaling reduces problematic alcohol use and protects against alcohol-related liver injury and steatotic liver disease progression via behavioral and metabolic pathways.
  • Genetically Proxied FGF21 Activity via Missense Instruments: Giontella et al. (PMID 37302695) leveraged the FGF21 missense variant rs739320—associated with MRI-derived liver fat reduction—as an instrumental variable, confirming that genetically proxied FGF21 activity protects against renal impairment, improves cardiometabolic profiles, and reduces hepatic fat accumulation.
  • Bidirectional MR and Metabolic Comorbidities: He et al. (PMID 40245929) evaluated causal associations across metabolic traits, showing that NAFLD, obesity, and type 2 diabetes significantly influence circulating FGF21 dynamics.
  • Behavioral Modulation and Macronutrient Regulation: Janzi et al. (PMID 41056811) and Flippo et al. (PMID 35108517) confirmed that FGF21 genetic variation and signaling modulate sweet-tasting sugar intake, macronutrient choice, and alcohol preference via central amygdalo-striatal circuits.

3.4 Integrated Human Genetics Verdict

(a) Level of Human Genetic Support for FGF21 in MASH: MODERATE.
Although the GWAS Catalog query surfaced zero primary CLEAN diagnostic associations (the catalogued locus associations are heavily dominated by off-topic dietary preferences, pQTLs, and subfraction lipid traits), published instrumental-variable and Mendelian randomization studies (PMID 36208799, PMID 41747774, PMID 37302695) provide robust human genetic evidence that genetically higher FGF21 activity causally reduces hepatic fat, improves liver function biomarkers, and mitigates liver disease progression.

(b) Constraint & Agonist Safety Margin:
FGF21 is completely unconstrained against loss of function in gnomAD v4 (LOEUF = 1.541, pLI = $3.34 \times 10^{-5}$), which indicates natural tolerance for reduced baseline endogenous expression. In an agonist development program, where the objective is pathway activation, gene constraint metrics do not flag safety liabilities. However, IntAct physical partner analysis reveals 11 interaction partners, placing FGF21 in the 11–20 partner category associated with an estimated 1.3-fold increase in clinical trial safety attrition, warranting careful dose optimization to avoid broad FGFR-mediated off-target and systemic effects.

(c) Isoform Considerations:
Human genetic and biological data do not support the existence of alternative functional isoforms in MASH pathogenesis; therefore, an isoform-selective strategy is neither viable nor required, and therapeutic efforts should remain centered on optimized, KLB-dependent FGF21 receptor agonists.

5. Target Safety Evaluation

5.1 Loss-of-Function Safety Profile

Overall verdict

STRONG support for the activation hypothesis, as human genetic variation and loss-of-function studies demonstrate that impaired FGF21 signaling exacerbates hepatic steatosis, dyslipidemia, insulin resistance, and dietary sugar/alcohol preference, indicating that therapeutic agonism directly restores essential metabolic defense mechanisms.

Adverse effects by system

Hepatic and Metabolic System

Human genetic variants associated with reduced FGF21 expression or function (such as common regulatory alleles rs838133 and rs2548957, as discussed by Daniel R Barros and Robert A Hegele) impair the suppression of hepatic lipogenesis and diminish stimulation of fatty acid oxidation (PMID 39450972, PMID 26654352). Clinically, carriers of hypomorphic or regulatory FGF21 alleles exhibit elevated circulating triglycerides, subclinical increases in hepatic fat accumulation, altered dietary macronutrient preferences, and impaired systemic glucose homeostasis. The severity of this dysmetabolic phenotype is moderate and largely reversible upon restoration of downstream signaling or pharmacological receptor activation (PMID 36618930, PMID 37848164). This adverse pathway does not represent a contradictory liability for agonists; rather, the metabolic deterioration caused by FGF21 loss validates that therapeutic agonism corrects underlying pathogenic lipid accumulation.

Pancreatic System

Endogenous FGF21 is highly expressed in the exocrine pancreas, where it functions as an essential autocrine and paracrine anti-inflammatory stress response factor (PMID 26654352, PMID 32029920). Loss of FGF21 signaling disrupts cellular proteostasis and secretagogue clearance, predisposing the tissue to acinar cell injury, experimental pancreatitis, and aggravated secondary inflammatory cascades (PMID 32029920). Severe hypertriglyceridemia associated with impaired FGF21-dependent triglyceride clearance further compounds the clinical risk of acute pancreatitis (PMID 37848164). The acinar-inflammatory mechanism is moderate to severe and potentially irreversible during necrotic episodes, whereas the hypertriglyceridemia-driven component is moderate and reversible with lipid-lowering therapies (PMID 37848164).

Cardiovascular System

Loss of FGF21 impairs multi-organ vascular protection, notably through downregulation of the angiotensin-converting enzyme 2 (ACE2)/angiotensin-(1-7) axis across renal and adipose tissues (PMID 29706566). In the absence of FGF21, experimental models demonstrate exacerbated angiotensin II-induced hypertension, adverse vascular remodeling, and endothelial dysfunction (PMID 29706566). Impaired cardiac FGF21 signaling also removes cardioprotective suppression of myocardial oxidative stress and pathological fibrosis, aggravating heart failure progression. The severity of vascular and hypertensive impairment is moderate and reversible upon replenishment of FGF21 signaling (PMID 29706566, PMID 41198096).

Central Nervous System and Ingestive Behavior

Hepatic FGF21 acts as an endocrine messenger to the brain to regulate reward circuitry and macronutrient preference (PMID 41238906, PMID 39450972). Genetic loss or attenuation of FGF21 signaling removes negative feedback on the consumption of simple carbohydrates and ethanol, leading to increased sugar consumption, sweet craving, and higher alcohol intake (PMID 41238906, PMID 39450972). This behavioral phenotype is mild to moderate in severity and fully reversible with dietary restriction or reconstitution of central FGF21 signaling.

Protective effects of LoF

FGF21 loss-of-function variants protect against bone mineral density loss and osteoclast-mediated bone resorption. Preclinical and clinical evidence indicates that elevated FGF21 signaling stimulates hepatic factors and endocrine pathways that accelerate trabecular and cortical bone resorption, predisposing to osteopenia and bone fragility (PMID 40594911, PMID 38902808, PMID 41198096). Consequently, natural loss of FGF21 signaling protects the skeletal system from high-turnover bone loss. This mechanism directly overlaps with the target pharmacology of FGF21 agonists, establishing bone density reduction as a key on-target safety parameter to monitor during high-dose agonist therapy.

Therapeutic window: heterozygous vs. homozygous LoF

Heterozygous loss-of-function states or partial reduction alleles (e.g., rs838133/rs2548957 effect alleles) produce mild-to-moderate metabolic shifts, including subtle increases in triglyceride levels, elevated preference for sweets, and subclinical changes in hepatic fat accumulation (PMID 39450972, PMID 41238906). Complete homozygous loss of function in preclinical models results in pronounced failure of hepatic lipid clearance during metabolic stress, marked susceptibility to pancreatitis, and accelerated vascular dysfunction under hypertensive challenge (PMID 26654352, PMID 29706566, PMID 32029920). For an agonist development strategy, partial restoration of FGF21 activity (equivalent to reversing heterozygous LoF deficits) is sufficient to achieve robust hepatic fat clearance, glycemic control, and reduction in circulating triglycerides (PMID 36618930, PMID 38782182). Maintaining exposure within this physiological-to-moderate pharmacological window avoids the supra-physiological signaling levels that trigger osteoclastic bone resorption and skeletal fragility, providing a well-defined therapeutic window for FGF21 agonism.

Marked reductions in trabecular and cortical bone mineral density, elevated skeletal fragility, and structural deterioration of bone microarchitecture are observed in states of chronic FGF21 elevation (PMID 22315431, PMID 38991938). Knock-in or transgenic animal model (transgenic mice overexpressing FGF21 and wild-type mice treated with pharmacologic FGF21, Wei et al., PMID 22315431). Somatic growth stunting, extended or arrested estrous cycling, anovulation, and female infertility are observed in conditions of continuous target overactivity (Wei et al., PMID 30389664). Mechanistically, unsuppressed hepatic FGF21 secretion—such as that driven by failure of HRD1-mediated polyubiquitination and degradation of the transcription factor CREBH—disrupts diurnal circadian rhythm behavior and downregulates the neuroendocrine pathways coordinating reproductive maturation and linear growth (Wei et al., PMID 30389664). Knock-in or transgenic animal model (FGF21 gain-of-function transgenic mice and liver-specific Hrd1-deficient mice, Wei et al., PMID 30389664). The endocrine adaptations that downregulate energy-intensive reproductive and somatic growth processes during starvation are coupled directly to FGF21-dependent metabolic rewiring.

Selectivity and isoform considerations

Pathological GoF phenotypes stem from the sustained systemic overactivity of canonical, full-length mature FGF21 signalling through its obligate co-receptor β-Klotho (KLB) in complex with fibroblast growth factor receptors, primarily FGFR1c (PMID 22315431, PMID 38991938). There are no distinct pathological splice variants or mutant neo-isoforms identified in the GoF literature; target toxicity is driven entirely by supra-physiological activation of the canonical pathway. For therapeutic agonist design, this indicates that engineering receptor-complex selectivity—specifically biasing molecule engagement toward adipose- or hepatic-enriched FGFR/KLB complexes while avoiding bone marrow or central nervous system engagement—is required to mitigate skeletal and neuroendocrine liabilities without losing metabolic efficacy.

Dose–response and target engagement implications

In human genetics, common regulatory variants near FGF21 (such as rs838133) alter circulating protein concentrations in a graded, allele-dose-dependent manner rather than inducing binary phenotypic shifts (Gallego-Durán et al., PMID 38894596). In transgenic and pharmacologic animal models, GoF phenotypes follow a graded dose–response relationship: intermediate exposure yields resistance to weight gain and clears hepatic lipid accumulation, whereas continuous high-dose exposure triggers osteopenia and reproductive suppression (Wei et al., PMID 22315431, Wei et al., PMID 30389664, Guillaume J Trusz, PMID 38991938). Because the therapeutic goal is receptor agonism, target engagement does not require continuous complete receptor saturation; instead, intermittent activation or partial agonism may decouple metabolic benefits from skeletal liabilities. Target engagement threshold: Unable to determine from available evidence. The single most important experiment to sharpen the therapeutic window is a dose-fractionation and exposure-duration study in rodents comparing intermittent versus continuous FGFR/KLB activation, measuring hepatic triglyceride clearance against longitudinal bone mineral density (by micro-CT) and circulating bone turnover markers (CTX-1 and P1NP).

Overall Safety Summary

In the context of an agonist therapeutic strategy, loss-of-function data validate that target deficiency exacerbates steatohepatitis and metabolic dysfunction, confirming the necessity of pathway activity. Genetic constraint metrics in gnomAD v4 show that FGF21 is TOLERANT to loss of function, with a LOEUF of 1.541 and a pLI score of $3.34 \times 10^{-5}$. A total of 20 high-confidence LoF variants pass quality filters in gnomAD, all of which are ultra-rare ($AF \le 6.17 \times 10^{-6}$), with zero variants existing at meaningful population frequencies ($AF > 0.01\%$). For an agonist approach, natural tolerance to LoF confirms that baseline physiological inhibition is viable, whereas gain-of-function models preview the primary on-target toxicities of supra-physiological receptor stimulation, specifically bone mineral density loss and endocrine disruption. Crucially, IntAct physical interaction partner analysis identifies 11 high-confidence interaction partners, placing FGF21 in the 11–20 partner category associated with an estimated 1.3-fold higher odds of clinical trial discontinuation due to safety (Razuvayevskaya et al., 2024). Consequently, the overall safety profile presents a manageable safety concern, provided that clinical dosing is calibrated to achieve metabolic clearance without crossing into supra-physiological thresholds that trigger skeletal resorption or neuroendocrine axis suppression.

8. Conclusions

The deterministic weighted rubric has computed an overall tractability score of 90.7% for FGF21 in metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis. This high score is anchored primarily by exceptionally strong preclinical proof-of-concept across diverse animal models and robust Mendelian randomization studies validating the causal metabolic benefits of elevated FGF21 activity. The primary on-target safety considerations center on supra-physiological bone resorption via PPAR-$\gamma$ activation and pleiotropic FGFR connectivity (11 IntAct partners, ~1.3x trial safety attrition risk), whereas direct small-molecule druggability and standardized IMPC mouse phenotyping data represent absent evidence domains. Overall, this assessment reflects an advanced, clinically validated therapeutic target whose pharmacological tractability is well-established through engineered biologic modalities.
